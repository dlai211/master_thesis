This thesis presents two independent studies: a GPU-based strip-clustering algorithm designed for the high-luminosity environment of the ATLAS experiment at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) and an optimization of an ATLAS Run 3 search for Higgs boson decays to dark photons.

The first study focuses on the development and validation of a strip-detector clustering algorithm in \testtt{Traccc}, a GPU-accelerated tracking toolkit (usable for both offline track reconstruction and the online trigger system) designed to outperform traditional CPU-based algorithms. To support increased data processing in a higher-collision-rate environment during the Phase-II upgrade program for the High-Luminosity LHC (HL-LHC), we propose Event Filter (EF) Tracking GPU pipelines that employ GPU-based algorithms. The strip clusterization algorithm groups cell hits from silicon strip sensors to form measurements and is integrated into the EF Tracking GPU pipelines in the \texttt{ATHENA} framework. The algorithm is validated and shows good consistency in comparing differences in local measurement positions between \texttt{TRACCC} and \texttt{ATHENA}. 

Alongside this detector-focused work, there is a contribution to a search for Higgs boson decays to photons + dark photons produced via gluon-gluon fusion. The search is based on an integrated luminosity of $135~\mathrm{fb}^{-1}$ from ATLAS Run 3 data. Motivated by finding a potential candidate for dark matter, we search for events with the final state of a Standard Model (SM) photon, missing transverse energy ($E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}$), 
and collimated sprays of hadrons called jets. My analysis aims to maximize the signal-to-background significance by optimizing selection criteria based on distributions of various variables. A Machine Learning (ML) classifier study, including Boosted Decision Tree (BDT) and Deep Neural Network (DNN) approaches, was also developed to further enhance signal sensitivity. After optimizing the selection criteria, the analysis improved the expected upper limit on the branching ratio of this process by $70\%$ (from $1.3$ to $0.75$) compared to the previous work.
